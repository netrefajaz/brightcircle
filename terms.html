What Bright Circle is, and isn't

Bright Circle offers general information, practical skills, and tools like check ins, a journal, and plans, for people who love someone with an addiction. Some of its family skills draw on the ideas behind CRAFT (Community Reinforcement and Family Training). The wording is our own, and we aren't affiliated with or endorsed by CRAFT's creators or any organization named in the app.

Bright Circle is not a substitute for a therapist, counselor, doctor, lawyer, or treatment program, and using it doesn't create a therapist, counselor, or other professional relationship with anyone. Nothing in the app is a diagnosis, a treatment plan, or advice about your specific situation. When you can, talk with a qualified professional about your family's situation.

Safety comes first

Bright Circle can't monitor you or your loved one, can't contact anyone for you, and can't respond to emergencies. If you or anyone else is in danger, call 911. If your loved one has ever hurt, threatened, or frightened you, please start with the app's "Stay safe" skill and reach out to an advocate before trying other skills, since some approaches, like stepping back or raising treatment, can make a dangerous situation worse.

Information about overdose and naloxone is general and follows public health guidance. Always follow the instructions that come with naloxone and from emergency responders.

Legal and medical topics

Some articles and skills mention topics where laws and rules differ by state and situation, such as housing an adult child, involuntary treatment, protective orders, custody, insurance, and consent to treatment for a minor. Bright Circle describes these in general terms only. For your situation, talk with a lawyer, legal aid office, or the right professional where you live.

No promises about results

Addiction and recovery are complicated, and no approach works for everyone. We can't promise that using Bright Circle, or any skill in it, will lead your loved one to change, cut back, stop, or enter treatment, or that it will improve your situation. Research on approaches like CRAFT describes what happened for groups of families in studies, not what will happen for yours.
